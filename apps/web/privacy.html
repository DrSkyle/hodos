Privacy

Updated 21 November 2026

Hodos uses your phone’s motion sensors and location to identify possible road defects. You can view the hazard map without enabling automatic mapping.

Location, motion, and route privacy

When automatic mapping is enabled, Hodos reads motion and location signals to recognize journeys and detect unusual road impacts. Analysis runs on your phone. Raw accelerometer samples and complete GPS routes are never uploaded.

On Android, Hodos trims the first and last 250 metres of every recorded journey so your home, workplace, and exact destinations stay off your saved trail. Public map aggregation quantises coordinates into H3 spatial cells rather than publishing individual driver traces.

While a journey is recorded, Hodos measures sustained vibration energy at each location fix to infer road surface type (asphalt, gravel, cobbles) and road quality. Those measurements, surface labels and quality scores stay on your phone and are not submitted.

Each road observation may include a location, time, GPS accuracy, estimated speed, hazard type, severity and detection score. Repair and clear-road reports can reference an existing hazard.

Live road feed (1-tap alerts) and voting

When you tap a live road alert (Police, Jam, Blocked, or Flood) in the web app, live map, or Android app, Hodos requests a live GPS fix (within ±150 m accuracy) to verify you are on that road before publishing. Live alerts carry no personal identifier and auto-expire automatically when stale or cleared by other drivers. Confirmation and repair votes are anonymous and rate-limited.

Derived insights

Road score, ride insights, surface shares, vehicle-wear estimates, deterioration rankings and smooth-route scores are computed on your phone from your own journeys. They are stored in local app storage, are never uploaded, and are erased when you clear app data or uninstall Hodos.

Identifiers

The app creates a random installation identifier and a separate identifier for each journey. The installation identifier persists in local app data and accompanies observations so repeated reports can be compared. It is not derived from your name, phone number or advertising ID. It is not proof of a unique physical device.

Storage

Observations are stored in Appwrite and are not publicly readable through the app. The aggregated hazard map is public.

Your phone holds a cache of map hazards, up to 50 journey summaries, journey trails with their recorded fixes and vibration readings, and any observations queued for upload in app-private SQLite storage. That queue does not currently use separate application-level encryption. Permanently rejected submissions are retained separately in private app storage until you clear them or remove app data. Observations queued before you turn mapping off can continue uploading in the background when network access returns.

The aggregation service examines the last 120 days of events. This is not a deletion schedule; older server observations are not currently deleted automatically.

Your controls

Turn off automatic mapping in Hodos or revoke location and activity-recognition permissions in Android settings. In the Mapping screen, after switching mapping off and finishing a journey, you can delete road observations that remain queued locally; requests already accepted by the server cannot be recalled. You can separately clear rejected reports and local journey history. Clearing local app data or uninstalling Hodos removes local information but does not erase observations already submitted to the server.

Promotions on the website

The website shows promotions for Hodos itself: the Garage, shop placements and the app. They are rendered from our own pages, so there is no third-party advertising script, no third-party cookie and no ad-network request. Choosing one takes you to a normal Hodos page, and from there the rest of this notice applies as usual.

Some shop links are affiliate links. If you allow it, a click on one is passed through our server to the affiliate network so the shop can credit Hodos. That shares your IP address, browser/user-agent string and the page you came from with that network; it does not include your journeys, your road data or your installation identifier. If you decline, the link still works and simply is not enriched. You can change your choice by clearing this site’s storage.

Nothing is ever placed over the live map or navigation view, and no ad-network promotion appears inside the web app at /app or in the Android app. The apps contain no advertising or analytics SDKs. If a third-party advertising network is ever used, this page will say so before it runs.

Hosting providers may process technical connection data necessary to operate the service.

The Garage, free shop pins, and the email waitlist

The Garage shows product offers fetched from a product catalogue through our own server, so no catalogue key and no personal identifier is sent from your phone or browser to look things up. Opening an offer leaves Hodos and goes to the retailer, whose own privacy policy applies from that point on.

Shop pins on the map are free initially everywhere with 0% commission and no fake listings. A shop supplies its own name, category, one line of text and its location; no driver data is shown to a shop, and pass counts are shown only as anonymous totals.

If you leave an email address for road notes or the referral-reopening waitlist, we store the address, the page or screen it came from and the date, so we can send occasional updates or notify you when referrals reopen and so the same address is not stored twice. Nothing connects that address to your journeys, your installation identifier or the map. Ask us to delete it and it is removed.

Contact

For data questions or deletion requests, contact the maintainers through the Hodos repository. Please don’t share precise location details in a public issue.
