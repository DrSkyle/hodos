# A hazard is published only once it is corroborated

A hazard reaches the public map only after at least three passes from at least
two distinct devices, and its confidence fades over time. This mirrors Boston's
Street Bump, which used a three-hit rule, and it is the whole reason the map can
be trusted without knowing who contributed. It also sets the shape of the data:
impacts are the unit of evidence, hazards are derived, and the threshold is a
tunable, not a constant buried in the code.
